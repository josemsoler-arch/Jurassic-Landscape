Choosing the Right Patio Materials for Arizona's Climate

Arizona's extreme temperatures and intense UV exposure can destroy some patio materials in just a few years. Learn which materials stand up to our climate and which to avoid.

Why Material Selection Matters in Arizona

Arizona's desert climate is brutal on outdoor materials. Summer surface temperatures can reach 150-180°F, causing materials to:

  • Crack from thermal expansion and contraction
  • Fade from intense UV radiation
  • Become too hot to walk on barefoot
  • Deteriorate from monsoon moisture followed by extreme heat
  • Shift or settle due to our expansive clay soils

Best Patio Materials for Arizona

1. Flagstone (TOP CHOICE)

Pros:

  • Stays relatively cool—up to 20°F cooler than concrete
  • Natural texture provides slip resistance
  • Unique, organic appearance
  • Extremely durable in Arizona's climate
  • Doesn't fade from UV exposure

Cons:

  • Higher initial cost ($15-30+ per sq ft installed)
  • Irregular shapes can make furniture placement challenging
  • May need occasional re-leveling

Best For: High-end landscapes, natural desert aesthetics, walkability in summer

2. Travertine

Pros:

  • Stays coolest of all patio materials—up to 30% cooler than concrete
  • Elegant, high-end appearance
  • Naturally slip-resistant when tumbled
  • Excellent durability in Arizona heat
  • Available in neutral tan/beige tones

Cons:

  • Most expensive option ($18-35+ per sq ft)
  • Porous surface requires sealing to prevent staining
  • Can be etched by acidic substances

Best For: Pool decks, luxury patios, areas with heavy foot traffic in summer

3. Concrete Pavers

Pros:

  • Wide variety of colors, shapes, and patterns
  • More affordable ($10-20 per sq ft installed)
  • Individual pavers can be replaced if damaged
  • Easier installation than natural stone

Cons:

  • Retains heat—can be very hot in summer
  • Colors may fade over time from UV exposure
  • Lower-quality pavers can crack in Arizona heat
  • Needs sealing to prevent staining and fading

Best For: Budget-conscious projects, shaded patios, areas that won't be walked on barefoot

4. Stamped/Stained Concrete

Pros:

  • Most affordable option ($8-15 per sq ft)
  • Seamless surface—no weeds or tripping hazards
  • Can mimic the look of stone or tile
  • Quick installation

Cons:

  • Retains significant heat—very hot in direct sun
  • Can crack from thermal expansion (common in Arizona)
  • Colors fade quickly from UV exposure
  • Difficult and expensive to repair cracks
  • Needs resealing every 2-3 years

Best For: Covered patios, budget projects, areas that won't be walked on barefoot

Materials to Avoid in Arizona

❌ Standard Concrete (Broom Finish)

Retains extreme heat, cracks frequently in Arizona's climate, and offers poor aesthetics. Use only if budget is severely limited and patio will be fully covered.

❌ Dark-Colored Materials

Black or dark gray pavers can reach surface temperatures of 180°F+ in summer. Avoid unless area is fully shaded.

❌ Ceramic or Porcelain Tile (Outdoor Grade)

While durable, tiles can crack from thermal expansion and become dangerously hot. Better suited for covered patios only.

Tips for Keeping Your Patio Cooler

  • Choose light colors: Tan, beige, and light gray reflect heat better than dark colors
  • Add shade structures: Ramadas, pergolas, or shade sails dramatically reduce surface temperature
  • Install misting systems: Can cool patio areas by 20-30°F
  • Use outdoor rugs: Provide cooler walking surfaces in frequently used areas
  • Plant strategically: Trees and tall shrubs provide natural afternoon shade

Our Recommendation

For most Gilbert, Queen Creek, and Mesa homeowners, we recommend flagstone as the best overall choice. It balances cost, durability, aesthetics, and heat resistance. For pool decks and high-use summer areas, travertine is worth the extra investment for its superior cooling properties.
